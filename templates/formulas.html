Worked through for Check values: OG 1.062, refractometer 1.044, correction factor 1.04.

Why the reading needs correcting

A refractometer measures how much the sample bends light. Its scale is calibrated on sugar dissolved in water, so it is accurate on wort before the yeast is pitched.

Once fermentation starts, the alcohol bends light too, and the reading stays higher than the real gravity. Knowing the OG lets the formulas tell sugar and alcohol apart. That is why every fermentation asks for it.

Your batch: the refractometer shows 1.044, but the beer is really at 1.0305.

1 · Gravity to Brix

The formulas work in degrees Brix, so both the OG and the refractometer reading are converted first:

Brix(SG)=−182.4601⁢SG3 {% call term("−", "775.6821", "SG", 2, false) %} {% call term("+", "1262.7794", "SG", 1, false) %} {% call term("−", "669.5622", "", 0, false) %}
Bo=Brix(OG)WCF Br=Brix(reading)WCF

WCF, the wort correction factor, makes up for wort not being pure sugar water. It is usually between 1.02 and 1.06, and 1.04 is the default. To find your own, measure unfermented wort with a hydrometer and the refractometer: WCF = refractometer Brix ÷ the Brix of the hydrometer's gravity. Set it in Settings.

Your batch: Bo = 14.64 °Bx, Br = 10.46 °Bx.

3 · ABV

ABV=(OG−FG)×131.25

The usual homebrew shortcut for alcohol by volume. It loses some accuracy for very strong beers.

Your batch: ABV = 4.14 %

4 · Apparent attenuation

Attenuation=OG−FGOG−1×100

How much of the sugar the yeast has eaten so far. It is "apparent" because alcohol is lighter than water and pulls the measured gravity down, so the figure is a little higher than the real one.

Your batch: 51 %

5 · Points left

Points=(FG−1)×1000

The corrected gravity in brewer's "points": 1.030 is 30 points. Useful for watching the last few points drop as fermentation finishes.

Your batch: 30 points

Warnings

  • Reading equals the OG: nothing has fermented yet, so there is nothing to correct.
  • Corrected gravity below 0.996: lower than beer usually gets. The OG or the reading is probably wrong.

The cubic formulas come from Sean Terrill, “Refractometer FG Results” (2011).
